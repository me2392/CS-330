This repository holds my final project for CS 330 at Southern New Hampshire University. For my 3D scene I recreated the bookshelf in my living room using C++ and OpenGL. It has a long black metal table with three wooden shelves, a large metal ring in the center, a round sandscape, two pumpkin candles, a black tray with candle holders, and shelves full of books. The 3D Scene ZIP folder has the full Visual Studio project, and the Design Decisions document explains the choices I made along the way.
1. What new design skills has your work on the project helped you to craft?
The biggest skill I picked up was learning to look at a real object and see the basic shapes inside it. Before this class I would have looked at my bookshelf and just seen a bookshelf. Now I see boxes for the legs, shelves, tray, and books, a torus for the metal ring and the sandscape frame, a flattened sphere for each pumpkin, and tapered cylinders for the candle holders. Even the candle flames are just small spheres stretched up into a teardrop. Once I started thinking that way, designing the scene felt a lot less overwhelming.
2. What design process did you follow for your project work?
I started with the big pieces and worked my way down to the details. First I got the floor, the four legs, and the three shelves in place, since everything else had to sit on top of them. Then I added the metal ring, the sandscape on the left of the top shelf, the candle tray in the middle, and the pumpkins on the right. The books came last, with a small stack lying flat on the top shelf and full rows on the middle and bottom shelves. I kept the proportions close to the real shelf and paid attention to small things, like pushing the ring out to a z of 1.55 so it cleared the front edge of the shelves at 1.4 instead of cutting through them.
3. How could tactics from your design approach be applied in future work?
Breaking something big into small, simple parts is something I can use on any project, not just graphics. I also learned to look for repetition early. All four legs are the same size, so I stored their positions in a list and drew them in one loop. The pumpkins and candle holders each have their own function that takes a position, so I could reuse them instead of rewriting the same code. I want to keep planning that way in future work, because it saved me a lot of time and made changes easier.
4. What new development strategies did you use while working on your 3D scene?
I split the scene into its own functions instead of putting everything in one long render method. There is a function for the floor, the bookshelf, the metal ring, the sandscape, the pumpkin candles, the candle tray, the candle holders, and the books. When something looked off, I knew exactly where to go. I did the same with the textures, materials, and lights by keeping each in its own setup function. If the wood looked too shiny, I only had to change it in one place. I also wrote a function to fill the middle and bottom shelves with books from leg to leg. It steps through a set of eight cover colors by three so the books next to each other are not always the same color, and it stops when the next book would go past the end of the shelf. That was a lot better than placing every book by hand.
5. How did iteration factor into your development?
Iteration was a huge part of this project. Almost every time I added something new, I had to go back and adjust something I had already done. When I added textures, the wood grain on the long shelves and the floor boards looked stretched, so I went back and scaled the UVs so the wood repeats four times across each shelf and the floor tiles instead of stretching. Lighting took the most rounds of changes. I ended up with a warm main light on the left like a lamp, a cool fill light on the right so nothing sat in full shadow, and a warm back light behind the shelf so its reflection would land on the floor and give it some shine. I also gave each material its own shininess, so the wood has a soft, wide highlight and the black metal has a small, sharp one.
6. How has your approach to developing code evolved throughout the milestones, which led you to the project's completion?
Early on I focused on getting each piece placed exactly right before moving on to the next one. By the end I was much more comfortable building something that worked, running it, looking at it, and improving it a little at a time. The milestones helped with that because each one added a new layer: shapes, then textures, then lighting, then camera controls. For the camera I set up WASD to move, Q and E to go up and down, the mouse to look around, and the scroll wheel to change the speed in small steps. P and O switch between the perspective and orthographic views. I also tilted the camera down a little at the start so the view would not jump the first time the mouse moved. Small fixes like that only came from running the scene over and over and paying attention to how it felt.

7. How do computational graphics and visualizations give you new knowledge and skills that can be applied in your future educational pathway?
This class showed me what actually happens behind the scenes when something shows up on a screen. I now understand how scaling, rotating, and moving an object works, how textures get wrapped onto a shape, how light and materials work together, and how the camera and projection change what you see. Switching between the perspective and orthographic views of my own scene made that click for me in a way that reading about it never did. I can carry these skills into future courses, especially anything involving user interfaces or interactive programs.
8. How do computational graphics and visualizations give you new knowledge and skills that can be applied in your future professional pathway?
I work in SAP support in a warehouse and logistics environment, and a big part of what I do is helping people see what is going on with the data. I have built a Power BI dashboard and a Power Apps scanning tool at work, and this class gave me a better appreciation for why color, lighting, layout, and how someone moves through what they are looking at matter so much. My goal is a software engineering role that lets me work remotely and travel, and being able to build clear, interactive visuals is something I can bring to any team I end up on
